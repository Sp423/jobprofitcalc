  <h1>What to Charge When a Frozen Pipe Bursts After Hours</h1>
  <div class="article-meta">
    <span>November 5, 2026</span>
    <span class="article-meta-sep">·</span>
    <span>11 min read</span>
    <span class="article-meta-sep">·</span>
    <span>JobProfitCalc.com</span>
  </div>
</header>

<section>
  <p>Plumbing repair on a burst pipe alone typically lands between $500 and $2,000, according to <a href="https://www.plumbingrepaircost.com/burst-pipe-repair-cost/">PlumbingRepairCost</a>, an aggregator of published U.S. national ranges last updated September 3, 2026. For the Minneapolis area, <a href="https://paulbunyanplumbing.com/blog/average-plumbing-repair-costs/">Paul Bunyan Plumbing &amp; Drains</a>, a Minneapolis-St. Paul plumbing company, reported emergency repairs of any kind at $500 to $2,500 or more in figures published August 3, 2026, with prices running higher during winter peak demand. Those published ranges tell you where the market sits, but your price for a 2 a.m. burst should be built from your own costs first and checked against them second.</p>
  <p>This guide shows a plumbing or small service shop how to price one emergency burst pipe ticket: the after-hours labor cost, the trip fee, the hours it takes to reach and fix the split, the parts, and the line where plumbing stops and water damage begins. Then it walks the numbers through the <a href="/calculator">JobProfitCalc calculator</a> with two worked examples. Every labor-hours figure, crew size, cost, and percentage in those examples is an assumed input. Replace each one with numbers from your own job records.</p>
  <p>One point up front, because it trips up a lot of shops. The published rates in this article are what plumbers bill customers. The calculator's Hourly Labor Rate field wants your loaded cost per hour, meaning wages plus payroll burden and benefits for the person on the job. Use the published billed rates only as a market check on the final price. Never type a billed rate into the labor field, or you will stack your margin on top of someone else's margin.</p>
</section>

<section>
  <h2>Why the 2 a.m. call costs more</h2>
  <p>A burst pipe does not wait for business hours, and the tech who answers it at 2 a.m. is giving up sleep, family time, or a shift premium that your shop has agreed to pay. That extra cost belongs inside the Hourly Labor Rate you enter, raised by an after-hours multiplier. The method for picking and justifying that multiplier is covered in <a href="/blog/after-hours-rate-for-contractors.html">how to set an after-hours rate for contractors</a>, so it is not repeated here.</p>
  <p>For context on what the market charges, <a href="https://www.plumbingrepaircost.com/burst-pipe-repair-cost/">PlumbingRepairCost</a> (U.S. national, updated September 3, 2026) reports that an after-hours or weekend call carries a multiplier of about 1.5x to 2x on the base labor rate. <a href="https://paulbunyanplumbing.com/blog/average-plumbing-repair-costs/">Paul Bunyan Plumbing &amp; Drains</a> (2026 national figures, published August 3, 2026) reports emergency rates at a 1.5x to 3x multiplier over standard hourly charges, after-hours weekday hourly rates of $120 to $300, and holiday or overnight hourly rates of $200 to $500. Those are billed emergency plumber rates. Use them to judge whether your finished price is in the neighborhood, and keep them out of the labor field.</p>
  <p>The practical move is simple: take your loaded daytime cost per hour, apply your after-hours multiplier to it, and enter that adjusted cost as the Hourly Labor Rate for this ticket only. Your regular daytime jobs keep their regular rate.</p>
</section>

<section>
  <h2>The trip fee stays on its own line</h2>
  <p>PlumbingRepairCost (U.S. national, updated September 3, 2026) reports that after-hours calls commonly add a flat trip or service fee of $100 to $300, and <a href="https://paulbunyanplumbing.com/blog/average-plumbing-repair-costs/">Paul Bunyan Plumbing &amp; Drains</a> reports a service call or diagnostic fee of $70 to $170 for the Minneapolis area (published August 3, 2026). Whether to charge, waive, or credit that fee toward the repair is its own decision, covered in <a href="/blog/should-contractors-waive-diagnostic-fee.html">should contractors waive the diagnostic fee</a>. In the examples below, the trip fee is quoted as a separate line and kept outside the calculator math so it is never counted twice.</p>
</section>

<section>
  <h2>Getting to the pipe is half the job</h2>
  <p>Most of the hours on a burst pipe ticket are spent before a single fitting goes on. <a href="https://www.plumbingrepaircost.com/burst-pipe-repair-cost/">PlumbingRepairCost</a> (U.S. national, updated September 3, 2026) reports that the fix on a frozen pipe, once it is reachable, is often $150 to $500, and that freeze bursts tend to happen in runs that are hard to get at: inside exterior walls, in crawl spaces, and in unheated attics. That gap between "reachable" and "not reachable" is where shops underprice.</p>
  <p>Your Estimated Hours should cover four separate chunks of work, and it helps to think about each one on its own before adding them together:</p>
  <ol>
    <li>Confirming the main or branch shutoff is closed and holding, which can take real time when a valve is seized or the curb stop is buried.</li>
    <li>Finding the split itself, since the visible water often shows up well downstream of the actual failure.</li>
    <li>Opening access by cutting a ceiling panel, pulling insulation, or opening a wall cavity, and protecting the area around it.</li>
    <li>Cutting out the damaged section, making the repair, restoring water, and watching the joint under pressure before leaving.</li>
  </ol>
  <p>None of these steps has a verified hour count behind it in this article. The hours used in the examples are assumptions to show the arithmetic. Pull your own averages from past freeze calls, and if you have no history yet, time the next few and write the numbers down by chunk.</p>
</section>

<section>
  <h2>Parts go in at cost</h2>
  <p>Pipe, fittings, couplings, push-to-connect repair fittings, solder, and any clamp you leave in place all go into Material Cost at what you paid for them. Keep a short list of the parts that show up on most freeze repairs so you can price from it quickly at night instead of guessing from memory. If a part comes off the truck stock, use its replacement cost, because that is what it will cost to restock.</p>
</section>

<section>
  <h2>Where the plumbing ends and the drywall starts</h2>
  <p>A burst pipe often leaves soaked drywall, wet insulation, and standing water behind it. <a href="https://www.plumbingrepaircost.com/burst-pipe-repair-cost/">PlumbingRepairCost</a> (U.S. national, updated September 3, 2026) reports that water damage cleanup commonly adds $1,000 to $5,000 or more and is separate from the plumbing. Keep it separate on your quote too. Opening access to reach the pipe is plumbing labor and belongs in your hours. Patching, taping, texturing, painting, drying, and mold prevention are a different scope of work, and they go on their own line or get referred to a restoration or drywall contractor.</p>
  <p>Folding that work into the plumbing ticket hides your real repair price, makes your numbers impossible to compare from job to job, and leaves you holding a scope you may not be set up to finish well.</p>
</section>

<section>
  <h2>How to price burst pipe repair, step by step</h2>
  <ol>
    <li>Confirm the main is off and the water has stopped before you price anything, because a still-running leak changes the scope every minute.</li>
    <li>Set the after-hours labor cost by taking your loaded daytime cost per hour and applying your after-hours multiplier.</li>
    <li>Add the trip fee as its own line on the quote, outside the calculator.</li>
    <li>Estimate hours to reach and fix the pipe, broken into shutoff, finding the split, access, and repair.</li>
    <li>Enter pipe and fittings at cost as Material Cost.</li>
    <li>Add Drive Time and Fuel / Travel Cost for the round trip; the reasoning for billing travel is in <a href="/blog/should-contractors-charge-for-drive-time.html">should contractors charge for drive time</a>.</li>
    <li>Run the calculator with your Overhead % and Desired Profit Margin.</li>
    <li>Compare the calculator price plus the trip fee with the published ranges.</li>
    <li>Split drywall and cleanup onto their own line or a referral before you hand over the quote.</li>
  </ol>
</section>

<section>
  <h2>Mapping the ticket to the calculator</h2>
  <p>Here is how each part of a burst pipe call lands in the <a href="/calculator">calculator</a>. For broader rate and markup questions across all plumbing work, see <a href="/plumber-job-pricing">plumber job pricing</a>.</p>
  <table>
    <thead>
      <tr><th>Calculator field</th><th>What to enter</th></tr>
    </thead>

      <tr><td>Estimated Hours</td><td>On-site hours for shutoff, finding the split, opening access, and the repair</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Number of Workers</td><td>How many techs are on site for the call</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Hourly Labor Rate</td><td>Your loaded cost per hour raised by your after-hours multiplier, never a published billing rate</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Material Cost</td><td>Pipe, fittings, and couplings at what you paid</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Material Markup (%)</td><td>0 in this article</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Drive Time</td><td>Round-trip hours to the job</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Fuel / Travel Cost</td><td>Fuel and vehicle cost for the round trip</td></tr>

  <p>Two mechanics matter for reading the results. Overhead applies to labor, drive labor, and materials at cost, and fuel is added after overhead. Desired Profit Margin then applies to the whole total cost, so the price covers every line before profit is set.</p>
</section>

<section>
  <h2>Example 1: a 2 a.m. burst in an open basement ceiling</h2>
  <p>This is an example. Every input below is assumed. Say a copper or PEX supply line splits in an unfinished basement ceiling at 2 a.m., and the joists are open so the run is easy to see and reach.</p>
  <ul>
    <li>Say the call takes 2.5 hours on site, covering shutoff, locating the split, cutting out the bad section, and making the repair.</li>
    <li>Assume a crew of one tech.</li>
    <li>Assume a loaded daytime labor cost of $45.00 per hour, raised by an assumed 1.5x after-hours multiplier to $67.50 per hour. The 1.5x is an example choice inside the 1.5x to 2x range <a href="https://www.plumbingrepaircost.com/burst-pipe-repair-cost/">PlumbingRepairCost</a> reports (U.S. national, updated September 3, 2026); set your own using the <a href="/blog/after-hours-rate-for-contractors.html">after-hours rate method</a>.</li>
    <li>Round-trip drive time is assumed at 0.5 hours, with an assumed $12.00 in fuel and travel cost.</li>
    <li>Pipe, couplings, and fittings are assumed at $38.00 at cost.</li>
    <li>Overhead is assumed at 20%, and the Desired Profit Margin is assumed at 35%.</li>
    <li>Quote Valid is assumed at 1 day.</li>
  </ul>
  <p>Enter materials at your cost. Your margin already earns profit on materials. If you also want a materials markup, use the optional Material Markup field: it adds profit on top of your margin, and the calculator shows your effective margin. This example leaves Material Markup at 0.</p>
  <table>
    <thead>
      <tr><th>Line</th><th>Arithmetic</th><th>Result</th></tr>
    </thead>
    <tbody>
      <tr><td>Labor</td><td>2.5 hours × 1 worker × $67.50</td><td>$168.75</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Drive labor</td><td>0.5 hours × 1 worker × $67.50</td><td>$33.75</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Materials at cost</td><td>Entered as assumed</td><td>$38.00</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Overhead base</td><td>$168.75 + $33.75 + $38.00</td><td>$240.50</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Overhead</td><td>20% × $240.50</td><td>$48.10</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Fuel / Travel Cost</td><td>Entered as assumed, outside overhead</td><td>$12.00</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Total cost</td><td>$240.50 + $48.10 + $12.00</td><td>$300.60</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Price</td><td>$300.60 ÷ (1 minus 0.35) = $300.60 ÷ 0.65 = $462.4615, rounded</td><td>$462.46</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Material markup</td><td>$38.00 × 0%</td><td>$0.00</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Profit</td><td>$462.46 minus $300.60</td><td>$161.86</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Margin earned</td><td>$161.86 ÷ $462.46</td><td>35.00%</td></tr>
    </tbody>
  </table>
  <p>Now add the trip fee as a separate quoted line. Assume $150.00, an example amount inside the $100 to $300 range <a href="https://www.plumbingrepaircost.com/burst-pipe-repair-cost/">PlumbingRepairCost</a> reports (U.S. national, updated September 3, 2026). It stays out of the calculator so it is not run through overhead and margin a second time. The customer sees $462.46 for the repair plus $150.00 for the trip, a combined $612.46.</p>
  <p>Compare that with the published ranges. <a href="https://www.plumbingrepaircost.com/burst-pipe-repair-cost/">PlumbingRepairCost</a> puts plumbing repair on a burst pipe alone at $500 to $2,000 (U.S. national, updated September 3, 2026), and <a href="https://paulbunyanplumbing.com/blog/average-plumbing-repair-costs/">Paul Bunyan Plumbing &amp; Drains</a> puts emergency repairs in the Minneapolis area at $500 to $2,500 or more (published August 3, 2026). The example lands near the low end of both, which makes sense for an open, easy-to-reach run.</p>
  <p>If your own calculated floor comes in below those ranges, recheck your hours and your after-hours multiplier, since low floors usually mean a missed chunk of access time. If your floor comes in above them, look at whether your loaded cost or overhead is out of line, or whether the job really is harder than a typical call. Either way, your floor is the number you should not go below; the ranges only tell you how far above it the market might carry.</p>
</section>

<section>
  <h2>Example 2: the same burst inside a finished exterior wall</h2>
  <p>Same house, same pipe size, same parts, same assumed inputs as Example 1, except this time the split is inside a finished exterior wall, a common spot for freeze bursts according to <a href="https://www.plumbingrepaircost.com/burst-pipe-repair-cost/">PlumbingRepairCost</a> (U.S. national, updated September 3, 2026). Say opening the wall cavity, protecting the floor, pulling wet insulation, and working in a tighter space adds an assumed 2 hours, bringing Estimated Hours to 4.5.</p>
  <table>
    <thead>
      <tr><th>Line</th><th>Arithmetic</th><th>Result</th></tr>
    </thead>
    <tbody>
      <tr><td>Labor</td><td>4.5 hours × 1 worker × $67.50</td><td>$303.75</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Drive labor</td><td>0.5 hours × 1 worker × $67.50</td><td>$33.75</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Materials at cost</td><td>Entered as assumed</td><td>$38.00</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Overhead base</td><td>$303.75 + $33.75 + $38.00</td><td>$375.50</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Overhead</td><td>20% × $375.50</td><td>$75.10</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Fuel / Travel Cost</td><td>Entered as assumed, outside overhead</td><td>$12.00</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Total cost</td><td>$375.50 + $75.10 + $12.00</td><td>$462.60</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Price</td><td>$462.60 ÷ 0.65 = $711.6923, rounded</td><td>$711.69</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Material markup</td><td>$38.00 × 0%</td><td>$0.00</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Profit</td><td>$711.69 minus $462.60</td><td>$249.09</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Margin earned</td><td>$249.09 ÷ $711.69</td><td>35.00%</td></tr>
    </tbody>
  </table>
  <p>Two extra hours of access moved the repair price from $462.46 to $711.69, a difference of $249.23, and the parts did not change at all. With the same assumed $150.00 trip fee on its own line, the customer sees $861.69 for the plumbing. That is still inside both published ranges, but it shows why a flat "burst pipe price" breaks down: the location of the split drives the ticket more than the split itself.</p>
  <p>Notice what this price does not include. Closing the wall, drywall patching, and drying out the cavity are quoted on a separate line or referred out. The only published figure here for that side of the job is <a href="https://www.plumbingrepaircost.com/burst-pipe-repair-cost/">PlumbingRepairCost</a>'s report that water damage cleanup commonly adds $1,000 to $5,000 or more (U.S. national, updated September 3, 2026). Do not guess at a drywall number inside the plumbing quote.</p>
</section>

<section>
  <h2>Edge cases that change the ticket</h2>
  <h3>A second split shows up after the water is back on</h3>
  <p>Freezing rarely damages just one spot on a run. If a second leak appears when you restore pressure, stop and treat it as added scope. Rerun the calculator with the extra hours and parts, tell the customer the change before you start, and get approval. Putting a line on every freeze quote that says additional splits found during testing will be priced separately saves an argument at 4 a.m.</p>
  <h3>The burst is behind a finished ceiling</h3>
  <p>This prices like Example 2: the access hours climb, the parts stay about the same. Ceilings add their own work, such as catching water held above the drywall and protecting finishes below, so estimate those hours on their own instead of borrowing the wall number.</p>
  <h3>The line is still frozen</h3>
  <p>If part of the run is still frozen, you can repair the visible split and still have a hidden one waiting to open as the line thaws. Price the thawing time as labor hours, and do not close anything up until the full run has thawed and held pressure. Say on the quote that the repair covers the damage found once the line was thawed and tested.</p>
  <h3>The call turns out to need only a thaw</h3>
  <p>Sometimes the pipe froze but did not split. Price it the same way with fewer hours and little or no material, and keep the trip fee line. The calculator handles a small ticket fine; what matters is that your after-hours labor cost and drive time still go in, since the 2 a.m. drive costs the same whether or not you find a split.</p>
  <h3>The customer asks you to fold drywall into the plumbing price</h3>
  <p>Hold the separate line. Offer to quote the patch as its own item if your shop does that work, or hand over a referral if it does not. Keeping the two apart lets the customer see what the plumbing actually cost and keeps your plumbing margin from quietly absorbing a scope you did not price.</p>
</section>

<section>
  <h2>Sanity checks and common mistakes</h2>
  <p>Before the quote leaves your hands, run a few quick checks against your own numbers.</p>
  <ul>
    <li>Look at the Hourly Labor Rate field and confirm it holds your loaded cost times your multiplier, not a billed rate like the $200 to $500 overnight figure <a href="https://paulbunyanplumbing.com/blog/average-plumbing-repair-costs/">Paul Bunyan Plumbing &amp; Drains</a> reports (2026 national, published August 3, 2026).</li>
    <li>Check that the trip fee appears exactly once, on its own line, and nowhere in the calculator inputs.</li>
    <li>Make sure access hours are in Estimated Hours, since leaving them out is the most common reason a freeze ticket comes in low.</li>
    <li>Confirm drive time was counted for every tech on the truck, because drive labor scales with crew size.</li>
    <li>Scan the quote for drywall, paint, or drying charges hiding in the plumbing total and move them to their own line.</li>
  </ul>
  <p>A few mistakes come up often enough to name directly. Shops apply the after-hours multiplier to the final price instead of the labor cost, which inflates parts, fuel, and overhead along with it; the fix is to raise only the Hourly Labor Rate. Shops add a markup on parts and then also apply margin, without realizing the margin already earns profit on materials; the fix is to keep Material Markup at 0 unless you mean to stack it. Shops price frozen pipe repair from the reachable fix range alone and forget the hours to get there; the fix is to break hours into the four chunks before adding them up. And shops treat a published range as a price list; the fix is to build your floor from your costs and use the range only as a check.</p>
  <p>Finally, keep records. After each freeze call, note the actual hours by chunk, the parts used, and where the split was. <a href="https://paulbunyanplumbing.com/blog/average-plumbing-repair-costs/">Paul Bunyan Plumbing &amp; Drains</a> notes that Minnesota frozen pipe calls rise between November and March (published August 3, 2026), and wherever your shop is, a single winter of tracked tickets will give you better hour estimates than any assumption in this article.</p>
</section>
