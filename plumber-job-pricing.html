Plumbing Pricing Guide

Diagnostics, Trip Fees, and Licensed Rate

Licensed plumbing work supports premium pricing because of diagnostic skill, urgency, and permitting — the same reason HVAC and plumbing contractors often command higher margins than commodity trades. A $400 plumbing repair does not get shopped the way a $12,000 remodel does. That advantage disappears if the quote is still hours times a familiar rate with no trip fee, no material run, and no tax.

This page is plumber job pricing in one place: how to price plumbing jobs that include diagnostics, a minimum trip fee, a return for parts, and permit time, then run the same numbers through the calculator above.

What hourly rate should a plumber charge?

Most licensed plumbers in mid-sized U.S. markets charge between $75 and $150 per hour for direct labor, with service calls often carrying a separate minimum trip fee of $100–$200. Your specific rate should be calculated from your income target and costs, not from what you've heard others charge.

That figure is the labor rate you enter in the calculator, not the whole job price. Overhead, materials, drive time, and taxes still sit on top. If you are building the rate from income and billable hours, use how to calculate your contractor hourly rate, then apply it here.

Always charge for diagnostic time

Always charge for diagnostic time — your expertise in finding the problem is worth money, not just fixing it. Diagnostic time is often where you earn your real hourly rate. A leak that takes twenty minutes to repair and forty minutes to find is an hour of licensed work, not a twenty-minute favor.

Minimum trip fee on short calls

Set and enforce a minimum trip fee for service calls under one hour. Quick calls that eat drive time are where small operators bleed margin. Customers already accept travel charges from plumbers, HVAC companies, and electricians every day. The contractors who skip the charge often haven't tested whether customers actually object.

Return trips for materials are billable

If a job requires a return trip for materials, that return is billable time. Include a material-run allowance on any job with custom or specialty parts. The homepage plumber tips put this next to diagnostics and trip fees because it is the same leak: unpaid time that never shows up in hours-times-rate.

Permits and inspections

Permit pulls, code compliance research, and inspection coordination take real time. Build an allowance into any permitted job before you quote it. Pass permit fees through (or add a modest handling charge). Never absorb them — they are a job cost that belongs on the customer's invoice.

What margin should plumbing work hit?

Most experienced contractors target a net profit margin of 15–25% after all costs and taxes. Twenty percent is the industry benchmark. Licensed specialty trades with a diagnostic component — HVAC, plumbing, electrical — command higher margins because urgency pricing applies and the supply of qualified providers is limited. Below 10% is a danger zone.
